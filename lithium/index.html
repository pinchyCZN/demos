LITHIUM

A 64k DOS demo dated 8 March 1997. 320x200 mode 13h, hand-written 386 assembly in real mode, and a tracker module that can be mixed in software or handed to a Gravis Ultrasound to mix in hardware.

It is built the same way EXCURSION is: a linear startup script that stores parameters into a block of globals and calls one runner per part, with no dispatcher table.

The original 63,281-byte LITHIUM.EXE is embedded in this page and unpacked in memory every time it loads. Nothing is pre-extracted and no unpacked image exists on disk: the meshes, the sine tables, the palettes and the module are all read out of the depacked bytes at startup, so what you are watching is driven by the 1997 file itself.

WHAT IT RUNS

Fifteen parts, in the original's order: a rock turning over the title, a terrain flyover, a dust cloud settling onto a bump-mapped surface, a tower that shatters and reassembles, a tunnel, a three-ship approach and battle, the tower falling, the greetings roll, and a stick figure running as the module ends.

The clock is the demo's own. Its timer runs at 57 Hz rather than a round number - the divisor in the image works out at 57.011 - and every part's length is a count of module rows rather than of frames, so the cuts land on the music the way they did on a 486.

THE PLAYGROUND IS OURS AND THE DEMO HAS NO SUCH THING. When the runner finishes, instead of looping it hands over to four pieces taken out of the demo and given controls: the flight, with a gun; the tunnel, with no end; the runner, orbited and zoomable; and the bump-mapped surface as a sheet to paint on with the mouse. Press g to go straight there.

KEYS

spacepause and resume
.single step one frame while paused
ffast forward, while held
[ ]jump a part back or on
gjump to the playground
dragswing the view, during any part
mmute
+ -volume, five steps

In the playground, 1 to 4 pick the piece:

1the flight - arrows steer, z fires, x c change speed
2the tunnel - the same controls, and it does not end
3the runner - drag to orbit, wheel or up/down to zoom, z x wind the walk either way, s toggles the smear
4the paint sheet - drag to draw, x c set how fast it dissolves, s stops the moving light, z wipes it

The drag in the demo's own parts only swings the view; it never touches what the demo is doing, and letting go eases back to the recorded camera. The playground's runner is the exception - its orbit holds where you leave it.
