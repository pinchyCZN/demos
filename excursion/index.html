EXCURSION

A 64k DOS demo by Vista Software, released at Abduction '96 and dated 6 June 1996. 320x200 mode 13h, hand-written 386 assembly, and a Scream Tracker module driving everything that moves - the camera speed in the opening is taken from two of the module's channel volumes, so the landscape coasts to a halt as they fade.

This is not an emulator. The original 49,761-byte XCURSION.EXE is embedded in this page and unpacked in memory every time it loads, and the demo's own data - meshes, palettes, sine tables, the module - is read straight out of that image. The code that draws it is a reconstruction in ANSI C, written from the disassembly.

A voxel landscape, a craft that launches from it, a planet approach, starfields, a dogfight, tunnels, plasma, a water simulation, and a drive across a grey moon. It runs about four minutes.

DRAG THE VIEW

Hold the left mouse button over the picture and the camera swings while the demo plays on underneath - nothing pauses and nothing is steered off its path. Let go and the view eases back to where the demo had it.

How it swings depends on what the part is made of. Over a landscape the camera turns where it stands, because the ground fills the frame at every bearing. In the dogfight and the pursuit it ORBITS: the view turns around the midpoint of the two craft, so they stay framed while you circle them and see their other sides. The departure takes a sideways swing only - that part uses a different renderer, with no horizon to move.

The starfield does not turn with the camera. The field is a torus in 16-bit space and its renderer takes a position rather than a bearing, so rotating it is a change to the projection rather than an addition to it. It is written up in the notes; it is not done.

KEYS

spacepause and resume, music and all
.single step one frame while paused
ffast forward, while held
[ ]step back and forward through the parts
mmute
+ -volume, five steps
mousehold and drag to swing the view

Stepping through the parts REPLAYS the module to get there rather than jumping, because a part is defined by the music position it waits for. Skipping a long way takes a moment.

KEYS - THE DRIVE

The demo ends on a black screen. What follows it is ours: the car from the moon part, made driveable, with the craft parked nearby to fly if you would rather.

zgas
xbrake, and the craft's airbrake too
left rightsteer
1swap between the car and the craft
up downcraft only - trim the nose, which is the climb
ccamera a quarter turn around
v bcamera closer and further
acamera height: hug the ground, or track the car
sground colour: the demo's, or the grey moon

The craft has no gravity and no suspension - it holds whatever the trim gives it, with the terrain as a floor and a ceiling above. The car has both, so a ridge taken fast will throw it.

A keyboard is needed for all of the above, so a phone will play the demo and let you drag the view, but not drive.
